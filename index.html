Classification and interpretation

The displayed is_desert_2026 field is preserved from the master dataset. Its stored results match a nearest-pharmacy distance threshold of greater than 1 mile where is_urban = 1 and greater than 10 miles otherwise. The latter currently includes both suburban and rural categories. This is not the USC suburban 2-mile rule.

Distances shown are stored centroid-based straight-line estimates, not driving routes. Population-weighting of the origins is not independently established here. Legacy drive-time estimates and PVI scores from the older pharmacy layer are withheld to avoid mixing analysis versions.

The density view shades only desert tracts with a continuous, log-scaled population-density overlay. Darker blue indicates greater density, not a validated priority score. The vulnerability view highlights the stored dual-burden flag; SVI ranking provenance and current location quality limit interpretation.

Geographic proximity does not establish hours, medication stock, public dispensing eligibility, delivery access, transportation options, or payer-network participation. A pharmacy-count difference between incompatible source lists is not evidence of confirmed closures.

From research to decisions

Target the next question, not just the next dot.

These are candidate investigation pathways, not demonstrated intervention effects or a plan-specific network-adequacy determination.

01

Validate geographic access

Audit addresses and shared coordinates, confirm public-facing dispensing, and test road-network travel from inhabited areas before selecting intervention sites.

02

Understand compounded barriers

Review vehicle availability, disability, language, and financial barriers alongside SVI. Evaluate transportation or delivery options without double-counting the same vulnerability domains.

03

Identify current payer partners

Use current CCO service areas and OHP enrollment context; overlapping and partial-county coverage require care. Actual network analysis needs participating-pharmacy directories and appropriately governed member data. OHA CCOs ↗ · OHP enrollment ↗

04

Connect with broader community need

Use the 2026 OHSU Areas of Unmet Health Care Need report and variables to contextualize local primary, dental, and mental-health access. This complements, rather than replaces, pharmacy-access analysis. OHSU resources ↗

About the project

RxAccess Oregon

A geospatial pharmacy-access research and decision-support project by Brandon San, MBA, MSHI, PharmD Candidate 2027, connecting health informatics, pharmacy practice, and equitable access.

Developed in the context of the University of Illinois Chicago Master of Health Informatics capstone and Pacific University pharmacy research. This public-data prototype is distinct from future governed payer analyses.
